How I think

  • A paywall on the first screen asks people to pay for a promise. A paywall after their first win asks them to keep something they already have. Those are very different requests.

  • Every project starts with one metric to move and one I refuse to hurt. If I can’t say which, I’m not ready to design yet.

  • Physiology taught me that attention runs out and willpower dips by evening. Your users meet your product at their least patient, so the right choice has to be the easy one.

How I use AI

Two ways: as a material I design with, and as a partner that makes me faster. In both, the rule is the same. AI drafts, a person decides.

In the products I design

  1. 1
    Explain before actingAI says what’s about to happen and why. It never sends, signs, or pays on its own.
  2. 2
    One look for AIEvery AI suggestion wears the same marker, so people always know what came from a model.
  3. 3
    Earn trust, then chargeAI features prove themselves for free first. The paid tier comes after people have felt them work.
  4. 4
    Know the limitsIn health, AI gives context and next steps, never a diagnosis. Anything urgent goes to a person.

In how I work

  1. 1
    Thinking partnerI pressure-test positioning, hypotheses, and copy with AI before anyone else sees them.
  2. 2
    Faster explorationI drive Figma with AI to try several directions quickly, then refine the chosen one by hand.
  3. 3
    Design to codeI build working prototypes with AI, so interactions are tested in a browser, not imagined.
